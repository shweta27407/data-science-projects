# Company and Industry Research for Data Science

Welcome to **Company and Industry Research for Data Science**.  
After completing this module, you will be able to:

- Explain how to research a company before an interview.  
- Recognize reliable sources for company and industry research.  
- Conduct an effective informational interview.  

---

## Why Research Matters
When you schedule a job interview, one of the best ways to prepare is by learning as much as possible about:

- **The organization** you’re interviewing with.  
- **The industry** you are aiming to enter.  

Thorough research will help you project confidence, use the company’s language effectively, and stand out among other candidates.

---

## Sources of Information

### 1. Company Website
The company’s official site is your first stop. Pay close attention to:

- **History** → Learn about the company’s origins, mission, vision, and culture.  
- **Keywords** → Identify repeated terms or phrases the company uses to describe itself. Use this language in your interview to show alignment.  
- **Careers Page** → Explore job listings to see where the company is growing or focusing efforts.  
- **Data & Reports** → Look for sales figures, customer numbers, achievements, or performance data. These can reveal the company’s tools, scale, and priorities.  

---

### 2. External Sources
Go beyond the company’s own materials:

- **Chamber of Commerce & Better Business Bureau** → Reliable sources of legitimacy and business standing.  
- **Social Media** → Offers insights into company culture, latest updates, and employee engagement.  
- **News & Web Searches** → Look for articles, interviews, and press coverage. This may reveal opportunities, challenges, or red flags.  

---

### 3. Professional Networks
Platforms and reviews can provide authentic, first-hand perspectives:

- **LinkedIn** → Research the company, and if possible, your interviewer’s professional background.  
- **Glassdoor** → Read employee reviews and interview experiences (check multiple posts for balanced insight).  

---

## Informational Interviews

### What They Are
An **informational interview** is not a job interview. Instead, it is an **informal conversation with a professional in the field** you want to pursue. Its purpose is to:

- Gain insights into careers.  
- Collect tips and expert advice.  
- Expand your professional network.  

---

### How to Arrange One
1. Make a list of **3–5 target companies** in your area of interest.  
2. Use LinkedIn, professional networks, or personal contacts to identify **data scientists** working at those companies.  
3. Reach out with a **brief introduction** and a clear purpose for your request. You may use:
   - Email  
   - Phone  
   - LinkedIn  
   - Academic or professional referrals  

---

### Conducting the Interview
- Let the conversation flow naturally.  
- Take notes, especially on advice and tips.  
- Respect the agreed time (ideally ≤ 30 minutes).  
- Afterward:
  - Review and highlight your notes.  
  - Send a thank-you note.  
  - Stay connected (LinkedIn, email, etc.).  

---

## Questions to Ask in an Informational Interview

### About Career Path
- How did you become interested in the data scientist role?  
- How did you prepare to enter the field?  
- How did you begin your career?  

### About Current Role
- What is a typical workday like for you?  
- What do you enjoy most about your work?  
- What has been your favorite project so far?  
- What is the most difficult challenge you’ve faced?  
- What would you like to accomplish next in your career?  

### About Career Advice
- What related fields or paths should I also consider?  
- How do you stay current and learn new technology?  
- What trends or issues in the field should I be aware of?  
- What strategies do you recommend for landing a position in this field?  

---

## Key Takeaways
- **Company websites** are your best starting point for research.  
- **External sources** (social media, news, Glassdoor, LinkedIn) give additional insights.  
- **Informational interviews** are powerful for building knowledge, gaining advice, and expanding your network.  
- Thorough research gives you the **confidence and credibility** to impress interviewers and differentiate yourself from other candidates.
