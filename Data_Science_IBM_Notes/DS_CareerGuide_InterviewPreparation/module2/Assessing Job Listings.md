# Assessing Job Listings

Welcome to **Assessing Job Listings**.  
After completing this module, you will be able to:

- Dissect and read job listings critically.  
- Describe the differences between **FTE (Full-Time Employee)** and **Contract** positions.  
- Navigate company application policies.  
- Recognize **problematic job listings** and avoid red flags.  

---

## Structure of a Job Listing

Most job listings are divided into sections. Each part provides valuable insights:

1. **Overview of the Company and Role**  
   - Broad description of the position and hiring organization.  

2. **Responsibilities and Tasks**  
   - Outlines day-to-day work, responsibilities, and team structure.  

3. **Requirements and Qualifications**  
   - Specifies the **educational background, certifications, experience, and skills** required.  
   - Includes both **required** and **preferred/optional** skills.  
   - Rule of thumb: Aim to meet all required qualifications and at least **50% of the preferred ones**.  
   - Identify missing requirements and consider how other strengths might compensate.

## FTE vs. Contract Positions

Job postings often specify whether the role is **full-time (FTE)** or **contract/contingent**.  
This distinction refers to **tax and employment status**, not just working hours.

### Characteristics of FTE Employees
- Work indefinitely (no fixed contract end).  
- Paid regularly (salary or hourly).  
- Must comply with company policies and training.  
- Eligible for company benefits and incentives.  
- Typically have stronger legal rights related to termination and labor protections.  

### Characteristics of Contract / Contingent Workers
- Work for a defined time period (as stated in contract).  
- Paid hourly or per deliverable.  
- Receive little to no training (expected to be skilled from the start).  
- Usually **not entitled** to benefits or incentives.  
- May be terminated without cause or notice, with fewer workplace protections.  

💡 **Both options have trade-offs**:  
- FTE = more stability, benefits, and structure.  
- Contract = more flexibility, but less security.

## Following Application Policies

Each company has its own process. Failing to follow instructions can disqualify your application.

- Many companies post on job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, Glassdoor) but **require applications through their own website**.  
- Common requirements:
  - Online forms.  
  - Supporting documents (resume, cover letter, portfolio link, work samples).  
  - Screening questions or skill assessments.  
- Some employers provide a direct **email or phone contact** instead.  

✅ Always read the posting carefully and follow instructions exactly.

## Warning Flags in Job Listings

Not every listing is worth applying to. Look out for red flags such as:

### 1. Lack of Clarity
- Vague job titles or descriptions.  
- Excessive jargon, acronyms, or unclear language.  
- Signals potential miscommunication or undefined role boundaries.  

### 2. Unrealistic Expectations
- Entry-level positions requiring many years of experience.  
- Listings demanding high-level expertise across too many unrelated areas.  
- Suggests the company doesn’t understand the role or intends to exploit new hires.  

### 3. Evident Bias or Prejudice
- Language that **excludes or favors candidates** based on irrelevant personal traits such as:
  - Race, religion, gender, age, marital or parental status.  
  - Orientation, nationality, military status, physical appearance, or abilities unrelated to the job.  

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Job listings** have structured sections that reveal both company and role expectations.  
- **FTE and contract positions** differ in benefits, security, and obligations—understand both before applying.  
- **Application processes vary by company**—follow them exactly to avoid disqualification.  
- Watch out for **red flags**: vague descriptions, unrealistic demands, or biased language.  
- Remember: A job listing is the company’s **first impression** on candidates. Clear, inclusive postings reflect professionalism and culture.
