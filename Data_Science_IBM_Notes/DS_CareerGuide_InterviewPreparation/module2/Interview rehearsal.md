# Interview Rehearsal

Welcome to **Interview Rehearsal**.  
After completing this module, you will be able to:

- Explain why it is important to rehearse for job interviews.  
- Describe the materials to develop and prepare ahead of time.  
- Rehearse verbal components of an interview effectively.  

---

## Why Rehearsal Matters
- You don’t need a scheduled interview to begin preparing.  
- Rehearsing gives you a **competitive edge** because many candidates skip this step.  
- Knowing information is not enough—you must be able to **present it smoothly and confidently**.  
- Practicing delivery bridges the gap between preparation and performance.  

> Rehearsal builds confidence, reduces stress, and makes you stand out.  

---

## Step 1: Develop Your Elevator Pitch
An **elevator pitch** is a short, 3–4 sentence introduction of yourself.  
It should include:
1. Your **name and title**.  
2. Your **recent experience**.  
3. What you’re **looking for in your next role**.  

Example:  
> “My name is Angela Menendez, and I’m a data scientist. For the past two years, I’ve worked in an entry-level role helping derive business insights from data using established models at an e-commerce company. I would like to move into designing machine learning models, and I’m seeking a mid-level position with opportunities to achieve that goal.”

✅ **Practice until natural**: Memorize, repeat aloud, and use it in both networking and interviews.  

---

## Step 2: Prepare for Common Interview Questions
Look up frequently asked questions and create **original answers**. Examples include:

- Why did you choose this field, and why are you interested in this job?  
- Tell us about a time you solved a problem on a project and how you did it.  
- Where do you see yourself in five years?  

Tips:
- Use your **resume** to recall skills, tasks, and accomplishments.  
- Practice **storytelling techniques** (e.g., STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result).  
- Speak answers aloud until they feel natural.  

---

## Step 3: Prepare Questions to Ask Employers
Remember: Interviewing is a **two-way street**.  

- Research the **company, product, and people** beforehand.  
- Ask questions such as:
  - What is the team’s work style (collaborative vs. independent)?  
  - What is the promotion path from this role?  
  - How does the company support career growth and skill development?  
- Practice asking them aloud so they sound confident and conversational.  

---

## Step 4: Rehearse Effectively

### Techniques
- **Mirror practice** → Observe body language, eye contact, and nervous habits.  
- **Record yourself** → Review tone, pacing, and clarity.  
- **Mock interviews** → Have a friend or mentor ask you real questions.  
  - Include both prepared and surprise questions.  
  - Practice staying calm and thinking on your feet.  

### Goals
- Sound **natural, not memorized**.  
- Get comfortable speaking from notes.  
- Build the ability to **adapt under pressure**.  

---

## Key Takeaways
- Rehearsal is your **secret weapon** in the interview process.  
- Develop and practice your **elevator pitch**.  
- Prepare and rehearse answers to **common interview questions**.  
- Draft and practice asking **your own questions** for the interviewer.  
- Use mock interviews, recordings, and self-review to improve.  
- Investing time in rehearsal reduces stress, boosts confidence, and helps you **stand out from other candidates**.
