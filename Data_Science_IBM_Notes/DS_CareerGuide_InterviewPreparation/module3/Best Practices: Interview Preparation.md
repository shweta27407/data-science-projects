# Interview Best Practices – Reflections from Cindy and Antonio  

**Cindy (Candidate):**  
I prepared for this interview like I would any other. The first step was reviewing the **job description** carefully and figuring out what I needed to know. If I wasn’t sure about something, I’d look it up.  

I also reviewed my **resume** to make sure I could explain every single point in detail. For example, if I had listed a programming language I hadn’t used in a while, I refreshed my memory before the interview.  

Since I already had background knowledge and experience, a lot of it was at the top of my head. That helped too. But I also practiced **practical programming questions** in advance.  

One of the biggest things I did was create a **cheat sheet** of fundamental concepts and common data science/machine learning interview questions. Before the interview, I’d review the list, practice answering, and even explain concepts out loud to the mirror.  

Sometimes I’d even record myself on my phone while practicing answers. That way I could review later and notice things like:  
- Bad posture at the start.  
- Talking too fast when I was nervous.  
- Stuttering when I wasn’t confident.  

This practice helped me refine my delivery and present myself better.  

---

**Antonio (Interviewer):**  
That’s excellent preparation. And you’re right—it’s normal **not to know the answer to every question**.  

When I conduct interviews, I tailor technical questions to the candidate’s level. If I notice your knowledge is shaky, I won’t push with advanced questions. But if you’re answering perfectly—like textbook answers—then it’s fun to push further and see where your limits are.  

The important thing is: not knowing everything doesn’t mean you won’t get the job. What matters is how you handle those moments.  

The best approach is to:  
1. Be honest if you don’t know.  
2. Offer an **educated guess** and explain your reasoning.  

That shows me how you think. For example, when asked about the **cost of training data** for linear vs. ridge regression, Cindy admitted she wasn’t sure but then reasoned her way to a thoughtful answer. That earns **brownie points** because it demonstrates problem-solving ability.  

But here’s the key: if you’re completely clueless and don’t have the fundamentals, **don’t fake it**. It’s always better to say, *“I’m not familiar with that,”* than to give a completely wrong answer.  

---

**Antonio – Practical Tips:**  
- **Arrive early.** Always plan to arrive at least 10–15 minutes before the interview. You never know what might delay you—traffic, transit, etc. Being early makes a positive impression.  
- **Plan your trip.** Don’t leave logistics to chance.  
- **Dress appropriately.** Match the company’s culture. For example, at Skills Network Lab, the culture is fun but still professional. So, dress cleanly and appropriately without being overly formal.  

---

## Key Takeaways
- **Preparation matters:** Review job descriptions, resumes, and technical concepts in advance.  
- **Practice delivery:** Use mirrors, recordings, or mock interviews to refine confidence and body language.  
- **Don’t fear tough questions:** Answer honestly, or provide an educated guess with reasoning.  
- **Never fake knowledge:** Admitting you don’t know is better than guessing wildly.  
- **Logistics count:** Arrive early, plan your trip, and dress in line with company culture.
