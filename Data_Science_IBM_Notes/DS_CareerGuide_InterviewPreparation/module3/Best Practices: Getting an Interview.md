# Post-Interview Reflections and Advice  

**Antonio Cangiano (Skills Network Engineering Manager & AI Specialist at IBM):**  
Whenever we post a Data Scientist position—especially internships—we receive a massive number of applications. Sometimes up to **1,000 candidates** apply, so much so that we have to shut down the application form early to avoid reaching 5,000.  

From there, it’s a challenge:  
- Narrowing it down to around **100 resumes**.  
- Scheduling interviews.  
- Ultimately selecting **3–5 candidates** for the final stage.  

In Cindy’s case, she did a lot right:  
- She had a **great resume tailored for the job**. She noticed the requirements and highlighted experiences that matched.  
- Her **cover letter** was friendly, professional, and well-phrased, which gave me a strong first impression.  
- She also **connected with me on LinkedIn**, sent me a message, and asked thoughtful questions. That showed genuine interest.  

Not every candidate does this, but it gave her an **edge**. When you’re one resume among hundreds, anything that makes you stand out in a positive way helps.  

My advice to other candidates:  
- **Tailor your resume** for each job. Don’t reuse the same generic version.  
- Keep a master document of all your experiences and skills, then pick the most relevant ones for each application.  
- Don’t hesitate to **connect with recruiters or hiring managers** on LinkedIn, ask questions, and show interest.  

---

**Cindy (Candidate):**  
I completely agree. Networking is very important, even if you feel shy about it. Many people in the industry are willing to share their expertise. If you show genuine passion, sometimes they might even **refer you to a job** or tell you about internal openings.  

Another piece of advice is: **don’t limit yourself to what you learn in university.**  
- University provides a solid theoretical foundation, but often doesn’t teach you how to apply those concepts in real-world industry settings.  
- You need to go beyond the classroom—work on projects, practice implementation, and learn tools used in the field.  

From my own experience:  
- Over the course of 3–4 months, I applied to more than **100 positions**.  
- Even though data science is in high demand, competition is fierce.  
- Out of those applications, I only got about **five interviews**, and IBM was fortunately one of them.  

For me, **LinkedIn** was the main platform:  
- It had many job postings.  
- It allowed me to follow influencers in data science and tech who shared their experiences and new opportunities.  
- Reaching out to Antonio on LinkedIn helped me stand out a little more, since it’s easy to get lost among 600 resumes.  

---

**Antonio:**  
Exactly. It’s not the only way—I’ve hired many candidates who never reached out on LinkedIn—but if you want a small edge, it’s worth doing.  

When I’ve already seen your name, answered your questions, and noticed your interest, I’ll naturally pay more attention when your resume shows up in the pile.  

In job hunting, it’s not only about **what you know**, but also **who you know**. Personal contact can:  
- Leave a good impression.  
- Give you insight into the role.  
- Increase your chances of being noticed and invited to an interview.  

---

## Key Advice from Antonio and Cindy
- **Tailor every resume** to the job description.  
- **Write a professional, friendly cover letter**.  
- **Use LinkedIn actively**: connect, message, and follow up.  
- **Network**—don’t be afraid to reach out, even to people you don’t know.  
- **Go beyond university learning**: practice real-world applications.  
- **Be persistent**: competition is tough, but consistency pays off.
